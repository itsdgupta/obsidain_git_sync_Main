# Fitts's Law

**Definition**
Fitts's Law dictates that the time required to interact with a target depends on the distance to the target and its size (closer and larger targets are faster to acquire).

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Maximize Target Size**
    *   Increase the size of interactive elements to reduce interaction time and user error rates.
    *   Combine icons with text labels to expand the overall active clickable area.
    *   Position frequently used menus on screen edges (for mouse-based interfaces) to create "infinite targets" where the cursor naturally stops, eliminating the need for users to slow down.

*   **Minimize Target Distance**
    *   Place sequentially related elements, such as a final form field and its submit button, close to one another to reduce pointer travel.
    *   Utilize rectangular (mega) menus or pie menus instead of long linear menus to reduce the average distance the cursor must travel to reach an option.
    *   Open contextual menus directly next to the user's current interaction point rather than at the far edges of the screen.

*   **Prevent Crowding**
    *   Maintain adequate spacing between small targets to avoid accidental clicks and overshooting.


# Hick's Law

**Definition**
Hick's Law states that the time it takes for a person to make a decision increases with the number and complexity of choices presented to them.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Reduce and Highlight Options**
    *   Minimize the total number of choices to decrease decision time.
    *   Highlight recommended or default options to prevent users from feeling overwhelmed.

*   **Break Down Complexity**
    *   Split complex or lengthy tasks into smaller, sequential steps to lower cognitive load.
    *   Use progressive onboarding to introduce features gradually to new users rather than showing everything at once.

*   **Avoid Over-Simplification**
    *   While minimizing choices is good, do not simplify the interface to the point of abstraction where users can no longer understand what actions do.



# Jakob's Law

**Definition**
Jakob's Law states that because users spend most of their time on other websites, they expect your site to function similarly to the ones they already know.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Rely on Existing Mental Models**
    *   Users transfer their expectations from familiar products to yours. 
    *   Using standard, recognizable design conventions allows users to focus on completing their tasks rather than learning how to navigate a new interface.

*   **Manage Change Gracefully**
    *   When rolling out major design changes, minimize user frustration by offering a transition period.
    *   Give users the option to preview the new design and temporarily revert to the older, familiar version until they adjust.


# Law of Prägnanz

**Definition**
The Law of Prägnanz states that people naturally perceive and interpret ambiguous or complex visual information as the simplest form possible in order to minimize cognitive effort.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Default to Simplicity**
    *   The human eye automatically organizes and transforms complex, overlapping shapes into a single, unified, and orderly form to prevent information overload.
    
*   **Enhanced Processing and Recall**
    *   Because simple figures require less mental effort to interpret, users can visually process and remember them much more easily than complex designs.


# Miller's Law

**Definition**
Miller's Law states that the average person can only keep about seven (plus or minus two) items in their working memory at any given time.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Organize Through Chunking**
    *   Break down and group complex information into smaller, manageable chunks to help users process, understand, and memorize it more easily.
    
*   **Avoid Rigid Rule-Making**
    *   Do not use the "magical number seven" as an absolute excuse to force unnecessary design limitations.
    *   Recognize that short-term memory capacity fluctuates based on the user's prior knowledge, familiarity, and current situational context.



# Peak-End Rule

**Definition**
The Peak-End Rule states that people judge an experience largely based on how they felt at its most intense point (the peak) and at its conclusion, rather than the average of every moment.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Optimize the Extremes**
    *   Focus your design efforts heavily on the most intense emotional moments and the final steps of the user journey.
    *   Identify when your product is most helpful, valuable, or entertaining, and deliberately design to maximize user delight during those specific peaks.

*   **Mitigate Negative Peaks**
    *   Be aware that users recall negative experiences more vividly than positive ones, making it crucial to eliminate severe friction points so they do not become the memorable "peak" of the experience.



# Aesthetic-Usability Effect

**Definition**
The Aesthetic-Usability Effect states that users often perceive aesthetically pleasing designs as being more usable and effective.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Positive Perception**
    *   Attractive designs trigger a positive emotional response, leading users to instinctively believe that the product works better simply because it looks good.
    
*   **Increased Tolerance & Masked Flaws**
    *   Users are far more forgiving of minor usability issues and friction when an interface is visually pleasing.
    *   *Warning:* Because of this tolerance, beautiful designs can accidentally mask underlying usability problems, making it harder to identify real user pain points during testing.



# Zeigarnik Effect

**Definition**
The Zeigarnik Effect states that people remember uncompleted or interrupted tasks much better than completed ones.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Visualize Progress to Drive Motivation**
    *   Display clear progress indicators (like progress bars or checklists) to leverage the brain's inherent desire to finish what it has started.
    *   Provide "artificial progress" or a head start (e.g., pre-filling the first step of a setup process) to increase the user's motivation to reach the finish line.

*   **Encourage Content Discovery**
    *   Provide clear signifiers or teasers for additional content (like truncating text with a "Read More" link), creating an incomplete loop that prompts users to explore further.


# Tesler's Law (Law of Conservation of Complexity)

**Definition**
For any system, there is a certain amount of inherent complexity that cannot be reduced or designed away.

**Key Insights (Combined for Easy Recall)**

*   **The Burden Shift:** Because a baseline of complexity will always exist, it must be dealt with by either the user or the system behind the scenes.
    *   *Takeaway:* Designers and engineers should do the hard work to shoulder this burden, absorbing the complexity so the user doesn't have to.
*   **Design for Reality:** Real users are rarely perfectly rational and won't always use the product exactly as intended.
    *   *Takeaway:* Rather than trying to force ideal behavior, accept the remaining complexity and provide accessible, contextual help (like tooltips) wherever the user gets stuck.


# Von Restorff Effect

**Definition**
The Von Restorff Effect (or The Isolation Effect) states that when multiple similar objects are presented together, the one that visually differs from the rest is the most likely to be remembered.

**Combined Key Insights**

*   **Highlight What Matters**
    *   Make important information or primary actions (like a "Buy Now" button) visually distinctive so they stand out from surrounding elements.
    
*   **Use Visual Emphasis Sparingly**
    *   Exercise restraint; if you emphasize too many elements, they will compete for attention and lose their isolation effect, or users might mistakenly ignore them, thinking they are banner ads.
    
*   **Design for Accessibility**
    *   Do not rely solely on color to create contrast, ensuring users with color vision deficiencies can still spot the difference.
    *   Be cautious when using motion or animation to create distinction, as this can negatively affect users with motion sensitivity.



# Doherty Threshold

**Definition**
Productivity soars when a computer and its user interact at a pace of less than 400 milliseconds, ensuring neither has to wait on the other.

**Key Insights**
*   **The 400ms Rule:** System feedback must occur within 400 milliseconds to hold the user's attention and maintain high productivity.
*   **Manage Perceived Time:** If a process takes longer than 400ms, use animations or progress bars to visually engage users and make the wait feel tolerable.
*   **Strategic Delays:** Purposefully adding a slight delay to a very fast process can sometimes increase its perceived value and instill user trust.
